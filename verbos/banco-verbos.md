# Banco de verbos

100 verbos semilla. **Datos, no lógica** — el método vive en `logica-verbos.md`.

**Cómo se lee este archivo:** con `grep`, solo las filas de la ronda. Nunca
entero (`logica-verbos.md` §9).

**Las frases modelo son para el coach**, para construir el estímulo y para
corregir. **Jamás se le enseñan a Jorge antes de que produzca** — eso convertiría
el ejercicio en reconocimiento (`logica-verbos.md` §2).

Categorías:
- `CONF` — confusables: el español los mete en una sola palabra y el inglés no.
- `PER`  — el verbo exacto que Jorge sustituye por una perífrasis de tres palabras.
- `PHR`  — phrasal verbs de frecuencia real alta.
- `OPI`  — verbos de opinión, análisis y desacuerdo (política / actualidad / OSINT).

**No entran aquí** los que ya vive `estructuras/banco-estructuras.md` (`manage to / fail to`,
`keep + -ing`, `put up with`, `tend to`, `come across as`, `get round to`,
`can't be bothered`, `end up + -ing`, `feel like + -ing`, `be worth + -ing`).
Se repasan desde su banco original: duplicarlos rompería la contabilidad.

## CONF — confusables (26)

| Verbo | Trampa | Frases modelo |
|---|---|---|
| borrow | tú **recibes**. `borrow sth FROM sb`. Nunca "borrow me" | I borrowed some money from my brother. · Can I borrow your charger for a sec? · I hate borrowing things from people at work. |
| lend | tú **das**. `lend sb sth` / `lend sth to sb` | She lent me her bike when mine broke. · I'd lend you the money, but I'm broke too. · Don't lend him anything, he never gives it back. |
| raise | **transitivo**: alguien sube algo | They raised the delivery fees again. · He raised his voice for no reason. · That raises an interesting question. |
| rise | **intransitivo**: sube solo. Nunca lleva objeto | Rents have risen a lot around here. · The sun rises at seven now. · Tension is rising between the two parties. |
| tell | `tell SB (sth)`. Siempre con persona detrás | She told me she was tired. · Don't tell my boss I left early. · Tell me what actually happened. |
| say | `say STH (to sb)`. Nunca "say me" | He said he'd be here by nine. · I didn't say anything to her. · What did she say about the shift? |
| bring | **hacia** donde está el hablante | Bring your laptop when you come over. · She brought me a coffee. · I brought it up during dinner. |
| take | **lejos** de donde está el hablante | Take this box to the third floor. · I'll take you home after work. · He took my charger and never gave it back. |
| remind | recordar **a otro** algo. `remind sb TO do` / `remind sb OF sth` | Remind me to charge the bike tonight. · That song reminds me of her. · She reminded me I'd promised to call. |
| remember | recordar **tú**. Sin objeto de persona | I can't remember where I left it. · Do you remember what he said? · I remembered halfway through the shift. |
| spend | `spend money/time ON sth` o `+ -ing` | I spent the whole morning waiting for orders. · She spends too much on delivery apps. · We spent Sunday doing nothing. |
| waste | tirar el tiempo o el dinero, con juicio negativo | Don't waste your time arguing with him. · I wasted an hour on a cancelled order. · That's a waste of money. |
| make | crear, producir, decidir. `make a decision / dinner / a mistake` | I made a mistake with the address. · She made us dinner. · Let's make a decision and move on. |
| do | actividad, tarea, trabajo. `do the dishes / homework / a job` | I did the dishes before she got home. · What do you do for a living? · He did a good job on that. |
| hear | te llega el sonido, sin querer | I heard something in the hallway. · I've heard about that case. · I couldn't hear you, the traffic was loud. |
| listen (to) | pones atención. **siempre `to` con objeto** | I was listening to a podcast the whole shift. · Listen to me for a second. · Nobody listens to what couriers say. |
| watch | seguir algo que se mueve o dura | We watched a film last night. · Watch the bike while I go in. · I've been watching how they handle the strikes. |
| look (at) | dirigir la mirada. **`at` con objeto** | Look at this message she sent me. · He looked at me like I was mad. · Just look at the numbers. |
| see | percibir, o entender | I saw her leaving the building. · I see what you mean. · I haven't seen him since Friday. |
| win | ganar **una cosa**: partido, premio, elección | They won the election by a narrow margin. · I won a free coffee on the app. · You can't win this argument. |
| beat | ganar **a alguien**. Lleva persona detrás | We beat them 3-1. · Nobody beats him at this. · They beat us on price, not on service. |
| earn | ganar **dinero** trabajando | I earn more on weekends. · She earns twice what I do. · He hasn't earned that reputation. |
| meet | conocer por primera vez, o quedar | We met at a party two years ago. · I'm meeting her at eight. · Nice to meet you. |
| know | ya conoces. **nunca "I know her yesterday"** | I've known her for two years. · I don't know that part of town. · He knows what he's doing. |
| steal | `steal STH (from sb)`. El objeto es **la cosa** | Someone stole my bike lock. · They stole the data from an open server. · He stole my idea in the meeting. |
| rob | `rob SB / a place`. El objeto es **la víctima** | They robbed a shop two streets away. · I got robbed on a night shift. · The whole system robs couriers of any security. |

## PER — el verbo exacto en vez del rodeo (26)

| Verbo | Trampa | Frases modelo |
|---|---|---|
| afford | **rodeo típico:** "I don't have money for" | I can't afford a new bike right now. · We can't afford to lose another client. · She can afford to say no; I can't. |
| mind | **rodeo:** "it's not a problem for me". `mind + -ing` | I don't mind working late. · Do you mind if I open the window? · She minded a lot more than she said. |
| avoid | **rodeo:** "I try not to". `avoid + -ing`, nunca `to` | I avoid riding at night. · She's been avoiding the subject all week. · You can't avoid talking about it forever. |
| assume | **rodeo:** "I thought that maybe" | I assumed you'd already left. · Don't assume everyone read the message. · People assume couriers choose their hours. |
| involve | **rodeo:** "it has a lot of" | The job involves a lot of waiting. · This doesn't involve you. · It involved rewriting the whole thing. |
| require | **rodeo:** "you need to have" — más formal que `need` | The app requires location access. · That requires a lot more evidence. · It doesn't require much effort. |
| mean | **rodeo:** "what I want to say is" | I didn't mean it like that. · That means we start at six. · What do you mean by that? |
| matter | **rodeo:** "it's not important". Casi siempre en negativa o pregunta | It doesn't matter now. · That's the only thing that matters to her. · Does it really matter who said it? |
| last | **rodeo:** "it takes / it continues during" | The shift lasted eleven hours. · Their arguments never last long. · The battery lasts about forty kilometres. |
| cope (with) | **rodeo:** "I can handle the situation" | I'm not coping very well with the night shifts. · He copes better than I do. · Nobody teaches you how to cope with that. |
| reckon | **rodeo:** "I think that". Muy común, informal, británico | I reckon she's right. · How long do you reckon it'll take? · I reckon they knew all along. |
| realize | **rodeo:** "then I saw that". Es darse cuenta | I realized I'd left the keys inside. · She didn't realize how bad it was. · It took me a year to realize that. |
| notice | **rodeo:** "I saw that". Es reparar en algo | I noticed she'd gone quiet. · Nobody noticed the change in the terms. · Did you notice how he answered? |
| wonder | **rodeo:** "I ask myself if" | I was wondering if you were free tonight. · I wonder what he really wanted. · She wondered why nobody said anything. |
| owe | **rodeo:** "I have to give him money" | I owe my brother two hundred euros. · You owe her an apology. · They owe us three weeks of pay. |
| deserve | **rodeo:** "it's fair that he has" | She deserves a lot better than that. · He didn't deserve any of it. · They deserve to know. |
| miss | echar de menos, **y** perder algo por poco | I miss having weekends off. · I missed the last train. · You missed the whole point. |
| rush | **rodeo:** "do it very fast" | Don't rush me. · I rushed the last delivery and got it wrong. · There's no rush. |
| hesitate | **rodeo:** "I doubted a bit before" | I hesitated before answering. · Don't hesitate to call me. · She hesitated just long enough for me to notice. |
| apply (for) | **rodeo:** "I sent a paper to ask for the job" | I applied for three jobs this week. · She applied for a visa in March. · That rule doesn't apply to us. |
| attend | asistir a. **falso amigo:** no es "atender" | I couldn't attend the meeting. · Only twenty people attended. · You have to attend the first session. |
| achieve | **rodeo:** "we got what we wanted" | We achieved almost nothing that quarter. · She's achieved a lot for her age. · Shouting achieves nothing. |
| improve | **rodeo:** "it's better now" | My English has improved a lot this year. · They promised to improve the conditions. · It improved once we talked about it. |
| complain | **rodeo:** "he said it was bad" | He complains about everything. · I complained to support and got nothing. · She never complains, which worries me. |
| warn | **rodeo:** "I told him to be careful" | I warned him about that street. · They warned us the app would change. · Nobody warned me it'd be like this. |
| refuse | **rodeo:** "he said no" | He refused to pay. · They refused my claim twice. · I refuse to work under those terms. |

## PHR — phrasal verbs de frecuencia real (30)

| Verbo | Trampa | Frases modelo |
|---|---|---|
| work out | resolverse, o calcular, o entrenar | It'll work out, don't worry. · I worked out how much I actually earn per hour. · Things didn't work out between them. |
| come up with | **rodeo:** "I thought of an idea" | She came up with a better plan. · I couldn't come up with anything on the spot. · They came up with that excuse in ten seconds. |
| put off | aplazar. **separable:** `put IT off`, nunca "put off it" | I keep putting off that conversation. · They put the meeting off until Monday. · Don't put it off any longer. |
| get away with | salir impune. **rodeo:** "nothing happened to him" | He got away with it because nobody checked. · They get away with paying us less. · You won't get away with that twice. |
| figure out | descifrar, entender por fin | I'm still figuring out how the algorithm assigns orders. · She figured it out before I did. · Let me figure this out. |
| deal with | lidiar con, encargarse de | I'll deal with it tomorrow. · She deals with customers all day. · Nobody wants to deal with that side of the job. |
| run out of | quedarse sin | I ran out of battery mid-shift. · We're running out of time. · They've run out of excuses. |
| get on with | llevarse bien, o seguir a lo tuyo | I get on with most of the other couriers. · Just get on with it. · They don't get on at all. |
| pick up | recoger, o aprender sin estudiar | I'll pick you up at eight. · I picked up most of my English from her. · Pick up the order and go. |
| turn out | resultar que | It turned out he'd known all along. · The night turned out better than expected. · Turns out nobody had read it. |
| go through | pasar por algo duro, o revisar | She's going through a rough patch. · I went through every message twice. · Nobody should go through that alone. |
| come across | encontrarse por casualidad | I came across an old photo of us. · I came across the leak on a forum. · You come across these cases all the time. |
| bring up | sacar un tema | I brought it up over dinner. · Don't bring that up again. · She brought up the money thing first. |
| sort out | arreglar, resolver | We sorted it out the same night. · I need to sort out my schedule. · They still haven't sorted the payment out. |
| take over | tomar el control | A bigger company took over the platform. · She took over halfway through. · The whole conversation was taken over by one topic. |
| catch up (with) | ponerse al día, o alcanzar | We should catch up soon. · I'm catching up on sleep. · The rest of the sector is catching up fast. |
| look into | investigar | I'll look into it and get back to you. · Nobody looked into the complaint. · They're looking into how the data leaked. |
| back up | respaldar (a alguien o unos datos) | Back me up on this. · There's nothing backing up that claim. · I back up everything twice. |
| call off | cancelar algo ya organizado | They called off the strike at the last minute. · We called the whole thing off. · Don't call it off now. |
| drop off | dejar algo o a alguien | I'll drop the package off on my way. · She dropped me off at the station. · Orders drop off after ten. |
| hang out | pasar el rato | We hung out at her place. · I don't hang out with them anymore. · Where do you usually hang out? |
| make up | inventarse, o reconciliarse | He made the whole story up. · We argued and made up the same night. · Couriers make up a big share of that workforce. |
| point out | señalar (en un argumento) | She pointed out that I'd said the opposite. · It's worth pointing out who funded it. · He pointed out the obvious flaw. |
| set up | montar, organizar | I set up the whole thing in an afternoon. · They set up a fake account. · She set us up on a call. |
| show up | aparecer | He showed up two hours late. · Nobody showed up to the meeting. · She always shows up when it matters. |
| stand out | destacar | That detail stood out immediately. · She stands out in any room. · Nothing about the profile stood out. |
| work on | trabajar en algo, mejorarlo | I'm working on my pronunciation. · We need to work on this together. · She's been working on it for months. |
| get by | apañárselas justo | I get by on what I earn, barely. · You can get by with basic English there. · We got by without a car for a year. |
| break down | averiarse, o venirse abajo | My bike broke down in the rain. · The talks broke down after a week. · She broke down halfway through telling me. |
| take up | ocupar espacio o tiempo, o empezar una afición | This is taking up my whole evening. · I took up running last winter. · It takes up too much space. |

## OPI — opinión, análisis y desacuerdo (18)

| Verbo | Trampa | Frases modelo |
|---|---|---|
| claim | afirmar **sin prueba**. No es "reclamar" | They claim the data was anonymised. · He claims he never saw the message. · The report claims otherwise. |
| argue | **sostener un argumento**, no solo discutir | I'd argue it's the opposite. · You could argue both ways. · She argued that the numbers were misread. |
| acknowledge | reconocer algo que te incomoda | They finally acknowledged the mistake. · I'll acknowledge he has a point. · Nobody acknowledged the report. |
| imply | insinuar sin decirlo | That implies they knew beforehand. · I wasn't implying anything. · What are you implying? |
| undermine | socavar poco a poco | It undermines the whole argument. · Those leaks undermined the case. · He kept undermining her in meetings. |
| address | abordar un problema. **falso amigo:** no es "dirigirse a" a secas | The law doesn't address the real problem. · She addressed it directly. · Nobody's addressing the cause. |
| tackle | atacar un problema de frente | You can't tackle that with more police. · They tackled the issue too late. · It's a hard problem to tackle. |
| highlight | poner el foco en algo | The case highlights a bigger pattern. · I'd highlight one detail. · It highlighted how little oversight there is. |
| dismiss | descartar sin considerar | They dismissed the whole thing as noise. · Don't dismiss it that quickly. · The claim was dismissed for lack of evidence. |
| blame | `blame SB FOR sth` / `blame sth ON sb` | They blamed the couriers for the delays. · Don't blame it all on the algorithm. · I don't blame her. |
| rely on | depender de, fiarse de | The whole thing relies on one source. · You can't rely on that platform. · I rely on tips more than on the base pay. |
| stem from | venir de, tener su origen en | Most of it stems from how they're paid. · The problem stems from the design itself. · Their distrust stems from experience. |
| account for | explicar, o suponer un porcentaje | That doesn't account for the missing hours. · Couriers account for most of the complaints. · How do you account for the gap? |
| back | apoyar públicamente | Two parties backed the proposal. · Nobody backed her publicly. · The evidence backs that reading. |
| enforce | hacer cumplir una norma | Nobody enforces those rules. · The law exists, it's just not enforced. · They enforce it selectively. |
| expose | destapar | The leak exposed the whole network. · It exposed how weak the controls were. · She exposed the practice publicly. |
| mislead | inducir a error | The headline is misleading. · They misled investors for years. · I don't think he meant to mislead anyone. |
| overstate | exagerar la importancia | I think that's overstating it. · The risk has been overstated. · You can't overstate how bad it was. |
